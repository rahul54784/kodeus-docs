Before launching execution, Warren requires you to configure capital allocation.

## What you configure

- Connected exchange
- Trading pair
- Allocation amount
- Execution boundaries

## Supported flows

- Allocation presets
- Custom allocation values
- Exchange-specific configuration
- Trading pair selection

Capital allocation is the final guardrail you set before a strategy goes live. Warren operates strictly within the amount, pair, and execution boundaries you define here.

## One capital pool, two books

Warren runs two kinds of execution: your **strategy bots** and Warren's own **discretionary book**. Both draw from a single shared capital pool, and a capital allocator holds margin reservations across the two so they can never double-spend the same funds. A bot and one of Warren's own positions cannot both claim the same capital at once.

The two books stay strictly separate everywhere else: their trades are stored, reported, and managed on their own, so the bots' numbers and Warren's numbers never blur together. See [Execution: Bots and Warren's Book](/docs/warren/execution) for how each book trades and the guards it carries.

<Callout type="info">
In paper mode, both books draw from a dedicated simulated budget instead of real funds, so you can practice the full allocation flow before any live capital is involved.
</Callout>

<Accordions type="single">
<Accordion title="Deep dive: how the pool prevents double-spending">

The capital allocator sits between your configured allocation and both execution books. When a bot opens a position or Warren opens one in its discretionary book, the allocator places a margin reservation against the shared pool. A second action cannot reserve capital that is already committed.

This is why allocation is a real guardrail and not just a display number: the boundary you set here is the ceiling both books are measured against, and reservations are what keep that ceiling honest across everything trading at once.

</Accordion>
