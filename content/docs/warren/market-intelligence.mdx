## What you can read

| Capability | What it returns |
| --- | --- |
| **Regime read, timeline & persistence** | The labeled regime with its cascade and coverage verdict, a rolling regime timeline, and a forecast of how long the current state is likely to hold (see [The Regime Engine](/docs/warren/regime-engine)). |
| **Technical indicators** | Standard indicators (RSI, MACD, Bollinger, EMAs, ADX, and more) computed on live closed-candle data for any pair and timeframe. |
| **Prices & charts** | A multi-pair snapshot (price, 24h change, high/low, volume) and raw candle history for charting or analysis. |
| **Why is it moving?** | A news and catalyst answer grounded in a fresh web search, not the model's memory. |

## Regime is the primitive

A **regime** is Warren's read of a market's current character: not where price is going, but what state it is in. Every read collapses to one of three basic directions, bullish, bearish, or ranging, refined into a finer seven-step ladder for graded conviction. It is reported with the *cascade* across three timeframes, 1h, 4h, and 1d: full agreement across all three is the strongest, most durable read, while a regime propped up by a single timeframe is fragile.

Because regime is the state the rest of the system conditions on, a market read is the natural first step before [building or backtesting a strategy](/docs/warren/building-and-backtesting) or acting on a [signal](/docs/warren/signals). The reasoning behind conditioning everything on regime is laid out in [Why regime-based backtesting](/docs/warren/why-regime-backtesting).

<Callout type="info">
A regime read is descriptive, not a fortune-teller. Warren is precise about the *state* of a market and honest about uncertainty: when the regime cannot be read with confidence, the [validity layer](/docs/warren/validity-layer) withholds a directional call rather than guessing.
</Callout>

## Deep mechanics

<Accordions type="single">
<Accordion title="Deep dive: closed-candle mechanics">

The most recent candle on any timeframe is still *forming*: its open, high, low, and close keep changing until the bar closes. Keeping that bar would leak the future into a read, so every accuracy-critical read drops it.

Warren fetches **one extra bar** and discards the still-forming last bar using a clock-skew-tolerant rule that *errs toward dropping*: if there is any doubt whether a bar has closed, it is thrown away (a just-closed bar is simply picked up on the next cycle), and a forming bar is never kept. Because the fetch is over-provisioned by one bar, indicators still receive their full lookback window rather than silently losing a bar of warmup.

Each read then carries three freshness fields, so downstream staleness checks are computations, not guesses:

```
freshness:
  is_closed      is the last returned bar fully closed?
  close_time     timestamp of that bar's close
  age_seconds    how old the read is, right now
```

Channels that are legitimately point-in-time, tickers, funding rate, and open interest, are labeled as live rather than forced through the closed-candle path, so a live number is never mistaken for a candle-close value. The result is that every read is reproducible and immune to look-ahead bias.

</Accordion>
